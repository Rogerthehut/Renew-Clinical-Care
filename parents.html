For parents and carers

Home with your child, with a nurse beside you.

Bringing your child home after surgery, treatment or illness can feel both reassuring and daunting. The proposed service is designed to bring calm, practical nursing support to children and their families following hospital discharge.

What you should be able to expect.

Clear explanations, respectful care and a plan that recognises your knowledge, preferences and family circumstances.

  • A named nurse and agreed visit schedule
  • Care explained in language that you understand
  • Respect for routines, privacy, culture and preferences
  • Written guidance on who to contact and when
  • Your consent before personal information is shared

You remain part of the team.

The aim is not to take over family life. It is to provide skilled support, answer practical questions and help you feel increasingly confident as your child recovers at home.

How it is intended to work

Simple steps, careful decisions.

1

Make an early enquiry

Tell us when your child’s discharge may happen, and how we can contact you. Do not send detailed medical information through the website.

2

Talk through the need

Once operational, a nurse would discuss the proposed support and whether a formal clinical review is appropriate.

3

Agree the plan

If the service is suitable, care could begin only after assessment, consent, handover and written agreement.

Questions worth asking before discharge.

  • What should recovery normally look like?
  • Which changes should prompt an urgent call?
  • Who retains clinical responsibility after discharge?
  • What medicines, wound care or equipment might be needed?
  • When is the next planned clinical review?

Need help now?

This pre-launch website is not monitored as a clinical service. It cannot provide diagnosis or urgent advice.

If your child is seriously unwell, call 999. For urgent medical advice, contact NHS 111 or your responsible hospital or clinical team.
